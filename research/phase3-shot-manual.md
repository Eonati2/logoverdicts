## The one thing that matters

**The clip must loop invisibly.** `is_looped: true` in 9 of the 12 cross-platform outliers
analysed. This is not a stylistic preference, it is the distribution mechanism: a viewer watches
a 5-second loop three or four times before consciously registering it, so watch-time per
impression multiplies, and the algorithm reads that as the strongest possible signal.

Every other rule below exists to protect the loop.

## Loop-ability changes the shooting order

The plan ranked shots by the view count of their precedent. That was the wrong axis. Since the
loop is the mechanism, what matters more is whether the action is **cyclic** (returns to its own
starting state) or **one-way** (consumes or depletes something and cannot return).

Cyclic actions loop for free. One-way actions fight you on every take.

| Loop class | Shots | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Free loop — start here** | Rotating surface (turntable), continuous pour | A full 360° rotation *is* the loop. An unbroken pour ribbon is quasi-periodic — start and end mid-pour and there is no seam at all. |
| **Loopable with discipline** | Trimming, dicing, skimming, dense cutting | Rhythmic and repeatable, but the output pile accumulates and the source depletes. Solved by framing, not editing. |
| **Fights the loop** | Peeling, seal-opening, single reveals | One-way by nature. Keep to 3–4s and accept a weaker loop, or skip. |

**Revised shooting order:** icing-on-turntable and thick-pour first (free loops, fastest to a
clean result), then spring onion trimming (highest ceiling in the data, worth the discipline),
then the rest.

## Shot cards

### 1 — Icing / ganache scrape on a rotating surface
*Precedent: @indalcakeofficial, 1M views · Loop class: FREE*

- **Frame:** top-down or 45°, turntable centred, scraper entering from one side.
- **Action:** hold the scraper perfectly still and rotate the surface beneath it. The pattern
  emerges from rotation, not from your hand.
- **Loop:** one full revolution returns the frame to its exact starting state. Cut on the
  revolution and the seam is mathematically invisible. This is the easiest true loop available
  to you.
- **Length:** 5–8s (one to two revolutions).
- **Fails when:** your hand drifts. Brace your wrist against the table, not in the air.

### 2 — Thick pour, unbroken ribbon
*Precedent: pattern-consistent across the set · Loop class: FREE*

- **Frame:** top-down into a **wide, shallow** vessel. Honey, syrup, condensed milk, batter.
- **Action:** pour from a consistent height so the ribbon coils on itself.
- **Loop:** start and end mid-pour, never showing the start or stop. The coiling ribbon is
  quasi-periodic, so any two mid-pour frames match closely. Width of the vessel is what keeps
  the level from visibly rising.
- **Length:** 5–8s.
- **Fails when:** the vessel is narrow — the rising level is a clock, and a clock kills a loop.

### 3 — Spring onion / herb trimming
*Precedent: CraftHarvest, **132.8M views**, 4s · Loop class: DISCIPLINE*

The highest-performing video in the entire sweep, and it needs a knife and a board.

- **Frame:** dark board, bunch aligned horizontally, blade entering from the right. Crop tight
  to the cutting zone only.
- **Action:** trim root ends in one rhythmic pass.
- **Loop:** frame so tightly that the offcut pile never enters shot, and keep an identical
  untrimmed bunch just outside the frame so the source appears infinite. End the clip the
  instant the blade lifts.
- **Length:** 4–6s.
- **Fails when:** offcuts accumulate in frame. Sweep between takes.

### 4 — Colour drop swirling into white
*Precedent: @artistic.rainbows, **21.4M views** · Loop class: DISCIPLINE*

- **Frame:** top-down into a bowl of white cream or batter, mixer hook or spoon rotating.
- **Action:** one drop of colour, slow rotation, colour resists and marbles.
- **Loop:** **stop before it incorporates.** The moment the mixture goes uniformly pink the loop
  is dead, because the end state no longer matches the start. Cut while it is still marbling.
- **Length:** 6–10s.
- **Fails when:** you over-mix. This is the single most common error on this shot.

### 5 — Skimming oil or fat off a dark liquid
*Precedent: @janetsilvabb1e, **10.3M views**, 11s · Loop class: DISCIPLINE*

- **Frame:** top-down, dark liquid filling the frame, ladle entering from one edge.
- **Action:** slow skim across the surface, breaking the film.
- **Loop:** use a **wide** vessel so the level drop is imperceptible across the clip.
- **Length:** 6–10s.

### 6 — Precision dice, extreme close-up
*Precedent: @viralfactsusa, 1.2M views · Loop class: DISCIPLINE*

- **Frame:** extreme close-up, blade and board only.
- **Action:** rhythmic dicing at a steady cadence.
- **Loop:** the cadence itself carries the perceived loop; the audio rhythm does more work than
  the image here. Keep the diced pile out of frame entirely.
- **Length:** 5–8s.

### 7 — Cutting something frozen or dense
*Precedent: @roshanmeatcuttingexpert, 749K views, 21s · Loop class: DISCIPLINE*

- Same rules as #6. The resistance sound is the draw — get the phone close.

### 8 — Seal, lid or wrapper opening in macro
*Precedent: @camew_motherhood, 938K views · Loop class: FIGHTS THE LOOP*

- One-way action, so keep it to 3–4s and treat it as a reveal rather than a loop. Lowest
  priority of the eight.

## Rig

The one purchase that matters: an **overhead phone mount** — a tripod with a horizontal arm or
a clamp arm, roughly $10–20. "Low (static camera)" appears in 8 of 12 winners and handheld
cannot deliver it. Books and tape will do for the first test only.

- Phone overhead, subject on a plain dark or plain white board.
- One soft light source from the side. Never from behind.
- **Lock exposure and focus before rolling.** Autofocus hunting mid-clip is a visible seam and
  it will kill an otherwise perfect take.
- Room as quiet as you can make it. Fridge off if it hums.

## Audio

Record the real sound and add no music. 8 of 12 outliers use raw ambient, and in this niche the
scrape and the blade *are* the content. Get the phone close; a phone mic 30cm from a cutting
board picks up detail that one at 1m does not.

## Rules that cost you the whole planet if broken

No face. No voice. No text overlay. No title card. All 12 outliers are tagged
`audience_is_global: true`, several explicitly "language-agnostic" — that absence of a language
gate is the *only* reason the ceiling here is 100M instead of 300K. Every English word you add
shrinks the addressable audience.

And: film only what you actually did. Reposting others' footage gets channels terminated, and a
daily cadence compounds that risk fast.

## Batch discipline

**Film one shot fifteen times, not fifteen shots once.** You cannot tell which take loops
cleanest until you have several to compare, and loop quality is the whole game. One two-hour
session should yield 10–15 usable clips — a week of daily posting with margin.

## Publishing

- Trim to the loop point, export 9:16, upload natively to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Reels.
- Caption: 3–5 broad tags only (`#satisfying #asmr #kitchen`). No sentences.
- One post per platform per day, same time daily.
- Stagger: do not dump the batch. Daily cadence is the variable being tested.

## Checking your work

Play every take on repeat before it ships. Watch for the seam. If you can see where it restarts,
the audience can too — and the loop is the mechanism, so a visible seam is the difference
between 5,000 views and 5,000,000.

Target: at least 3 of 15 takes should loop invisibly. If none do, the camera is moving or the
subject is depleting. Re-shoot before publishing anything.
